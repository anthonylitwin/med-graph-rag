Association of fenofibrate therapy with cardiovascular events and mortality in diabetes patients with early-diagnosed hyperlipidemia

The age at hyperlipidemia diagnosis has been reported to be associated with cardiovascular disease and mortality. This exploratory post hoc analysis of the Action to Control Cardiovascular Risk in Diabetes (ACCORD) Lipid trial investigated whether the age at diagnosis of hyperlipidemia influences the effect of adding fenofibrate versus placebo to simvastatin on cardiovascular outcomes and all-cause mortality in diabetes patients. Participants (n = 3861) were stratified into early- (≤ 60 years old) and late-diagnosed (> 60 years old) hyperlipidemia group based on self-reported year of hyperlipidemia diagnosis. Fenofibrate significantly reduced all-cause mortality risk in the early-diagnosed group (HR = 0.57, 95% CI 0.41–0.79) but not in the late-diagnosed group (HR = 1.12, 95% CI 0.81–1.55; P for interaction = 0.006). Similarly, fenofibrate reduced major adverse cardiovascular events (MACEs) risk in the early-diagnosed group (HR = 0.69, 95%CI 0.55–0.88), with a non-significant trend in the late-diagnosed group (HR = 0.86, 95%CI 0.64–1.16; P for interaction = 0.256). Notably, the reduction in MACEs risk was driven primarily by a significant decrease in death from cardiovascular causes among early-diagnosed hyperlipidemia group (HR 0.47, 95% CI 0.30–0.74; P for interaction = 0.07), whereas no significant benefit was observed for nonfatal myocardial infarction or stroke. These findings suggest that fenofibrate use is associated with lower cardiovascular events and mortality risk specifically in diabetes patients with early-diagnosed hyperlipidemia.

Introduction

Cardiovascular disease remains the leading cause of morbidity and mortality globally, with hyperlipidemia serving as a pivotal modifiable risk factor. Although traditionally viewed as a condition of middle-to-older age, hyperlipidemia is increasingly diagnosed among younger populations. These younger-onset cases can exhibit distinct pathophysiological features, including accelerated vascular injury, senescence-associated secretory phenotypes, and premature atherosclerosis leading to excess mortality. These suggest that the age at diagnosis of hyperlipidemia may critically modulate future cardiovascular risk.

Statin is the cornerstone of lipid-lowering therapy, demonstrating robust cardiovascular risk reduction effects in patients with diabetes. However, residual risk persists, particularly among those with atherogenic dyslipidemia (elevated triglyceride [TG] and low high-density cholesterol [HDL-c]). Fenofibrates, which activate peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor-α (PPAR-α), have multiple actions concerning cardiovascular risk management, including modulation of TG and HDL-c profiles and exerting pleiotropic anti-inflammatory effects. Despite such positive effects, clinical trials of fenofibrate have shown heterogeneous results in preventing cardiovascular events, highlighting the need to identify potential phenotypic or genotypic subgroups that may gain benefit from fenofibrate therapy beyond currently recognized responder populations.

Emerging evidence indicates that the age at diagnosis of hyperlipidemia is a key determinant of therapeutic efficacy. Hyperlipidemia diagnosed at a younger age is characterized by prolonged lipid exposure, aggressive plaque evolution, and heightened inflammatory activity, whereas cases diagnosed late in life may reflect age-related metabolic adaptations with attenuated vascular responsiveness. PPAR-α signaling, central to fenofibrate’s mechanism, also demonstrates an age-dependent decline, suggesting that younger patients with preserved pathway activity might benefit more. However, there is insufficient evidence on whether the age at diagnosis of hyperlipidemia influences the cardiovascular effects of fenofibrate. Thus, the objective of the current study was to determine whether the age at diagnosis of hyperlipidemia influences the treatment effect in the Action to Control Cardiovascular Risk in Diabetes (ACCORD) Lipid trial, which tested adding fenofibrate therapy vs. placebo to simvastatin, on the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACEs) and all-cause mortality.

Research design and methods

ACCORD-Lipid study design

The ACCORD Trial was a randomized, double 2 × 2 factorial design study that evaluated the effects of intensive glycemic control, intensive blood pressure control, and combined fenofibrate plus statin treatment on the prevention of cardiovascular disease (CVD) in people with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM). The detailed protocol, study design, and primary results of the ACCORD-Lipid trial have previously been reported.

Data collection and outcomes

Age at time of hyperlipidemia diagnosis was derived by subtracting the duration of hyperlipidemia from age at study enrollment (both in years). The inquiry of disease histories such as hyperlipidemia in the form was based on a previously physician-diagnosed condition without specifying the exact type of dysregulated lipid, reflecting real-world clinical practice. Serum concentrations of TG, total cholesterol (TC), HDL-c, LDL-c, and glucose were measured using the ACCORD central laboratory located at the University of Minnesota using an enzymatic assay (Roche, Indianapolis, IN). At each study visit and location, blood pressure was measured 3 times from the right arm with participants seated using an automated device (OMRON HEM-907); the average of the three readings was used as the visit blood pressure. Weight and height were measured by trained staff to calculate body-mass index (BMI). Data on demographic characteristics, lifestyle factors, medical history, and medication use were also collected through interviewer-administered standardized questionnaires.

Outcomes were independently evaluated by a central committee whose members were blinded to study group assignments based on the predefined criteria. The primary outcome was the first occurrence of MACEs (including nonfatal myocardial infarction, nonfatal stroke, or death from cardiovascular causes) and all-cause mortality.

Statistical analysis

Continuous data were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) when normally distributed, or median with interquartile range when nonnormally distributed, and categorical data as numbers (percentage). Between-groups difference regarding baseline characteristics was calculated using t-tests or Kruskal-Wallis tests for continuous variables and χ² tests for categorical variables. Since less than 5% of the data were missing across any baseline variables, these values were imputed using a simple imputation with predictive mean matching.

To assess whether the treatment effect of fenofibrate versus placebo on MACEs, death from cardiovascular causes, all-cause mortality varied across the continuum of age at hyperlipidemia diagnosis, we first examined the interaction between treatment assignment (fenofibrate vs. placebo) and the continuous age at diagnosis variable within the Cox proportional hazards (PH) model. Given the potential for a non-linear relationship, we further modeled the age at diagnosis using restricted cubic splines (RCS) and included an interaction term between the spline transformed age at diagnosis and treatment assignment. We also plotted the adjusted hazard ratios (and their 95% confidence intervals) for fenofibrate versus placebo across the range of observed ages at diagnosis (Fig. 1).

The cut-off point of 60 years of age for defining ‘early-’ versus ‘late-diagnosed’ hyperlipidemia was determined based on the RCS analysis and considerations of clinical applicability. The RCS plot revealed a transition in the treatment effect of fenofibrate around this age, characterized by the hazard ratio confidence interval crossing 1.0. We therefore selected 60 years of age as a conservative, round-number threshold that captures this transition while maintaining clinical utility (Fig. 1). To evaluate the robustness of findings, we conducted sensitivity analyses using tertiles of age at diagnosis and another threshold of 65 years.

Triglyceride change (ΔTG) was defined as the difference between TG levels at the last available follow-up and baseline. To assess whether the observed age-dependent treatment effects were independent of TG lowering, we conducted a series of sensitivity analyses. Participants were stratified by tertiles of ΔTG and by binary categories (ΔTG < 0 vs. ΔTG ≥ 0), and treatment effects were examined within each stratum according to age at hyperlipidemia diagnosis.

The events between treatment groups were compared using Cox PH model among participants in the early- and late- diagnosed groups separately. Several subgroup analyses were performed by sex, history of CVD, and previous statin use. All models, unless specified, was adjusted for baseline age, gender, education, smoking status, alcohol consumption, systolic blood pressure, fasting plasma glucose, BMI, history of CVD, glucose-lowering treatment, statin use, TG, HDL-c, LDL-c, duration of hyperlipidemia, and duration of diabetes.

To test the robustness of our findings, we repeated the analysis using specific events, including nonfatal myocardial infarction, nonfatal stroke, and CVD death. We also repeated the analysis regarding CVD death using Fine-Gray models by treating non-CVD death as the competing event.

All statistical analyses were performed using R software version 4.2.2 (R Core Team, Vienna, Austria). Differences were considered significant if P < 0.05.

Results

Baseline characteristics

Among 5518 participants enrolled in the ACCORD-Lipid trial, 1657 were excluded due to missing data on the self-reported ‘year of hyperlipidemia diagnosis’, either in those without hyperlipidemia history or in those with unknown or unreliable year of diagnosis, leaving 3861 participants finally included (Supplementary Fig. 1). As shown in Tables 1 and 2529 participants (65.5%) had early-diagnosed (≤ 60 years) hyperlipidemia and 1332 participants (34.5%) had late-diagnosed (> 60 years) hyperlipidemia. Baseline characteristics were well-balanced between the fenofibrate and placebo arms within each subgroup. The early- diagnosed group had a mean age of 59.4 ± 5.0 years, included 31.4% female, and had a CVD history of 41.1%. The prevalence in this group was 15.5% for elevated LDL-c, 59.7% for elevated TG, and 75.6% for low HDL-c. The late-diagnosed group had a mean age of 68.7 ± 4.7 years, included 28.5% female, and had a CVD history of 37.6%. Their dyslipidemia profile showed 13.8% with elevated LDL-c, 50% with elevated TG, and 71.7% with low HDL-c. Minor imbalances in LDL-c, HbA1c and elevated LDL-c were observed between treatment groups in the late- diagnosed group.

Effect of Fenofibrate on blood lipids

By the end of the clinical trial, in the early-diagnosed hyperlipidemia group, patients treated with fenofibrate showed a reduction in mean LDL-C from 99.3 to 82.3 mg/dl, an increment in mean HDL-C from 37.6 to 40.8 mg/dl, and a reduction in median TG from 174 to 121 mg/dl. Patients treated with placebo experienced a reduction in mean LDL-C from 99.0 to 81.9 mg/dl, an increment in mean HDL-C from 38.0 to 39.8 mg/dl, and a reduction in median TG from 167 to 144 mg/dl. In the late-diagnosed hyperlipidemia group, fenofibrate-treated patients showed a reduction in mean LDL-C from 93.2 to 75.0 mg/dl, an increment in mean HDL-C from 38.5 to 40.3 mg/dl, and a reduction in median TG from 144 to 108 mg/dl. Meanwhile, patients treated with placebo reported a reduction in mean LDL-C from 97.2 to 75.6 mg/dl, an increment in mean HDL-C from 38.6 to 40.1 mg/dl, and a reduction in median TG from 154 to 123 mg/dl (Fig. 2 & Supplementary Tables 1&2).

Primary analysis

Kaplan-Meier analysis revealed that in the early-diagnosed hyperlipidemia group, fenofibrate significantly reduced the incidence of MACEs (P = 0.004) and was also associated with a reduction in all-cause mortality (P < 0.001). In contrast, no significant treatment effects were observed in the late-diagnosed group (Fig. 3).

Comparing the effects of fenofibrate and placebo on primary outcomes in diabetes patients with early- vs. late-diagnosed hyperlipidemia. Shown are the cumulative incidence of the MACEs (A, B), and all-cause mortality (C, D) in patients with early-diagnosed vs. late-diagnosed hyperlipidemia

Results from the Cox PH model showed a significant interaction between treatment groups and the diagnosis age regarding all-cause mortality (P interaction = 0.006). Among participants with early-diagnosed hyperlipidemia, those allocated to receive fenofibrate had a 43% lower mortality risk compared with receiving placebo (HR 0.57; 95% CI 0.41–0.79; P < 0.001), while no significant association was observed among the late-diagnosed participants (HR 1.12; 95% CI 0.81–1.55; P = 0.486). Regarding MACEs, no significant interaction was found (P interaction = 0.256). The beneficial effect of fenofibrate to reduce MACEs was only present among patients in the early-diagnosed group (HR 0.69; 95% CI 0.55–0.88; P = 0.003) and not among patients in the late-diagnosed group (HR 0.86; 95% CI 0.64–1.16; P = 0.332) (Table 2).

Subgroup analysis

Regarding all-cause mortality, in the early-diagnosed group, the beneficial effect of fenofibrate on reducing all-cause mortality appeared more pronounced in males (HR 0.59; 95% CI 0.41–0.86; P = 0.006) compared to females (HR 0.53; 95% CI 0.27–1.05; P = 0.067), although the interaction was not significant. Similar pattern was found regarding prior CVD history, with consistent benefit observed in patients with prior CVD (HR 0.56; 95% CI 0.36–0.86; P = 0.009) and without CVD (HR 0.60; 95% CI 0.36–0.99; P = 0.048). Treatment effects also seemed to be more pronounced in statin users (HR 0.50; 95% CI 0.34–0.75; P < 0.001) than that in statin-naïve counterparts (HR 0.78; 95% CI 0.44–1.41; P = 0.416). However, no significant interaction was detected across subgroups defined by CVD history (P interaction = 0.823) or prior statin use (P interaction = 0.217). In the late-diagnosed group, the treatment effect on all-cause mortality did not vary significantly across any subgroups (Supplementary Fig. 2).

Regarding MACEs, in the early-diagnosed group, significant treatment heterogeneity was observed by sex (P interaction = 0.029), with fenofibrate reducing MACEs risk in males (HR 0.62; 95% CI 0.47–0.82; P < 0.001) but not in females. Among patients with prior CVD history, the risk reduction was significant (HR 0.72; 95% CI 0.54–0.98; P = 0.037), whereas no significant benefit was observed in CVD-free individuals. However, no significant interaction was detected between treatment groups and CVD history (P interaction =0.91). In addition, prior statin users showed benefit (HR 0.71; 95% CI 0.54–0.94; P = 0.018), although the interaction was insignificant. In the late-diagnosed group, males showed modest benefit (HR 0.71; 95% CI 0.51–0.99; P = 0.043), while no benefit was observed in females (P interaction = 0.005). No significant effects were observed in CVD history subgroups or previous statin use subgroups (Supplementary Fig. 2).

Sensitivity analysis

To assess the robustness of our primary findings, we conducted sensitivity analyses using alternative categorizations of age at hyperlipidemia diagnosis. When participants were stratified by tertiles (Supplementary Table 8), the association between fenofibrate treatment and clinical outcomes was consistent with the primary analysis. Significant risk reduction occurred in tertile 1 (26–54 years old) for MACEs (HR 0.69; 95% CI 0.49–0.97; P = 0.03), with a consistent, non-significant trend for all-cause mortality (HR 0.72; 0.45–1.17). The benefit persisted in tertile 2 (55–60 years) across all endpoints but was absent in tertile 3 (61–78 years). Consistent with these findings, a statistically significant interaction between age at diagnosis and treatment assignment was observed for all-cause mortality (P interaction = 0.013). Similarly, when using the 65-year threshold (Supplementary Fig. 4), the results were consistent with our main analysis. However, no significant interaction was found between the age at diagnosis of hyperlipidemia and lipid treatment when using the 65-year cut-off point.

To assess whether the age-dependent associations were driven by differences in TG-lowering intensity, we conducted sensitivity analyses stratified by tertiles of ΔTG (Supplementary Fig. 5). The first tertile represented patients with the greatest reduction in TG levels(most negative ΔTG), whereas the third tertile included those with the smallest or no reduction. Across ΔTG strata, the observed associations mirrored those seen in the primary analysis. Among patients with early-diagnosed hyperlipidemia, fenofibrate treatment was associated with favorable outcomes across ΔTG tertiles, with statistically significant reductions in both MACEs and all-cause mortality among patients with a moderate reduction in TG levels. In the third ΔTG tertile, fenofibrate treatment showed a favorable trend toward lower all-cause mortality among early-diagnosed patients, with a significant age-by-treatment interaction (P interaction = 0.012), whereas no benefit was observed among late-diagnosed patients. Similar patterns were observed when ΔTG was dichotomized as TG reduction versus no reduction: fenofibrate was associated with significantly lower risks of MACEs and all-cause mortality among early-diagnosed patients with ΔTG < 0 (P interaction for all-cause mortality = 0.039), but not among those without triglyceride reduction (Supplementary Fig. 6).

Regarding the individual components of MACEs, fenofibrate significantly reduced CVD death but showed no significant difference versus placebo in the risk of nonfatal myocardial infarction or nonfatal stroke (Supplementary Table 3). A borderline significant interaction was found between the age at hyperlipidemia diagnosis and lipid-lowering treatment for CVD death (P interaction = 0.07) (Supplementary Table 3), that the beneficial effect of fenofibrate in reducing CVD death was observed only in the early-diagnosed (HR 0.47; 95% CI 0.30–0.74; P = 0.001), but not in the late-diagnosed (HR 0.87; 95% CI 0.55–1.40; P = 0.573) hyperlipidemia group.

To account for the competing risk of non-CVD mortality, we further conducted a competing risk analysis, and the results remain robust (Supplementary Table 4).

Discussion

The current study examined whether the effect of fenofibrate-simvastatin combination therapy on cardiovascular and mortality outcomes in T2DM is modified by the age at hyperlipidemia diagnosis. We observed differences in the association between fenofibrate treatment and primary outcomes among patients diagnosed with hyperlipidemia at younger versus older ages. Compared with simvastatin alone, adding fenofibrate was associated with a lower risk of all-cause and CVD mortality in patients diagnosed at a younger age, but not among those diagnosed at an older age. Fenofibrate treatment was also associated with a lower incidence of MACEs in patients with an earlier diagnosis, although the interaction did not reach statistical significance for this endpoint.

Although few studies have investigated the potential role of age at hyperlipidemia diagnosis in lipid-lowering treatment, its relationship with cardiovascular risk is widely recognized. Both observational and Mendelian randomization studies have found that hyperlipidemia in young adulthood increases the risk of coronary heart disease, myocardial infarction, CVD death, and all-cause mortality. For example, the Copenhagen City Heart Study reported that high TG and low HDL only increased the risk of CVD in individuals who aged younger than 55 years. This age-dependent vulnerability aligns with findings from the Kailuan Study, which demonstrated that individuals with new-onset atherogenic dyslipidemia displayed a 2.61-, 1.41-, and 1.30-fold increased risk of CVD versus their matched controls at age < 45 years, 45 to 54 years, and 55 to 64 years, respectively. Results from The National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey study (n = 3148; age 30–59 years) also underscore the critical importance of initiating lipid-lowering therapy early in individuals with hyperlipidemia, particularly in individuals younger than 50 years of age. Thus, the age at diagnosis of hyperlipidemia holds clinical significance for guiding tailored therapeutic approaches, underscoring the need for further research to define optimal intervention strategies across different diagnostic ages.

In contrast with the original ACCORD-Lipid trial which reported that fenofibrate and simvastatin combination therapy could not reduce the risk of incident MACEs and all-cause mortality compared with simvastatin alone, here we found that patients diagnosed with hyperlipidemia at a younger age appeared to experience greater benefits from fenofibrate treatment. These findings extend prior ACCORD observations indicating that a uniform treatment approach may not capture treatment heterogeneity across patient subgroups. It complements the work of Ferreira et al.24, who used glucose control to stratify heart failure risk, by introducing an earlier diagnosis of dyslipidemia as a complementary dimension for reducing mortality. Together, these studies support the potential value of adopting multidimensional phenotyping in fenofibrate therapy. In this context, it should be noted that hyperlipidemia encompasses heterogeneous phenotypes. The present study focuses on dyslipidemia commonly observed in patients with type 2 diabetes, whereas familial hypercholesterolemia represents a distinct genetically determined disorder that requires dedicated diagnostic and management strategies and is beyond the scope of this analysis.

The differential efficacy of fenofibrate treatment in patients diagnosed with hyperlipidemia at younger versus older ages may reflects distinct pathophysiological stages of atherosclerosis. Hyperlipidemia diagnosed earlier in life exhibits accelerated plaque progression driven by pronounced lipid oxidation and inflammatory infiltration in youth, evidenced by autopsy studies showing 82% of young patients (< 40 years) display inflammatory eccentric lesions versus stable plaques in older adults. Fenofibrate’s dual modulation of lipid metabolism (via PPARα-mediated upregulation of lipoprotein lipase/ApoAV and ApoCIII suppression) and inflammation is particularly effective during this dynamic phase when PPAR-α signaling remains robust. While reduced PPAR-α expression and stabilized plaque architecture with aging may limit therapeutic responses in general populations. In complement to this, our findings demonstrate that although early-diagnosed hyperlipidemia confers elevated cardiovascular risk, it paradoxically exhibits superior therapeutic responsiveness to fibrate therapy, yielding greater cardiovascular benefits. The cardioprotective effects of fenofibrate in early-diagnosed hyperlipidemia, which persist after adjustment for HDL-c and TG levels, highlight the critical importance of therapeutic timing for simultaneously addressing metabolic and inflammatory drivers of atherosclerosis, suggesting that this phenotype may represent a distinct target population for personalized fenofibrate therapy. Notably, early- and late-diagnosed hyperlipidemia differed in several baseline clinical characteristics beyond age, including metabolic profiles, lipid levels, and disease duration. However, extensive multivariable adjustment and a series of sensitivity analyses—including stratification by ΔTG and alternative diagnosis age categorizations—consistently demonstrated that the age-dependent association between fenofibrate treatment and clinical outcomes was largely preserved. These findings suggest that the observed treatment heterogeneity is unlikely to be explained solely by baseline clinical differences, but rather reflects a clinically meaningful modification of fenofibrate efficacy by the timing of hyperlipidemia onset.

Sensitivity analyses stratified by the magnitude of TG reduction yielded results consistent with the primary findings. Across both tertile-based and binary ΔTG analyses, the clinical benefits of fenofibrate remained largely confined to patients with early-diagnosed hyperlipidemia, with significant age-by-treatment interactions for all-cause mortality, suggesting that age at diagnosis captures disease-related factors beyond lipid lowering alone. Notably, the sensitivity analysis showed that, within the younger subgroup, a reduction in cardiovascular events was observed only among patients who experienced a decrease in TG levels, whereas no benefit was evident among those without TG reduction. This observation suggests that the occurrence of TG lowering itself, rather than the magnitude of reduction, may be relevant to the observed clinical benefit in this subgroup. These findings highlight the importance of considering TG changes when interpreting the effects of fenofibrate therapy among younger patients with early-diagnosed hyperlipidemia.

In addition to disease stage–dependent mechanisms, the greater reduction in clinical events observed among statin users may reflect several non–mutually exclusive factors. Patients receiving statins before trial entry may have had lower cumulative LDL cholesterol exposure, conferring a more favorable baseline cardiovascular risk profile. Furthermore, pharmacokinetic interactions between fenofibrate and certain statins may enhance lipid-lowering effects, as simvastatin and other lipophilic statins are metabolized by cytochrome P450 3A4 (CYP3A4), and fenofibric acid may reduce statin clearance by influencing this pathway. Although these mechanisms could not be directly assessed in the present study, they may partly explain the greater treatment benefit observed among statin-treated participants.

Our results suggest that hyperlipidemia diagnosed at a younger age may warrant further investigation with respect to fenofibrate treatment response, yet current guidelines fail to delineate management protocols specific to the age at diagnosis. Bridging this knowledge gap could optimize personalized prevention and help reduce residual cardiovascular risk. Our findings provide a better understanding of the benefit-risk profile of fenofibrate and emphasize the importance of individualized treatment strategies. Nevertheless, these observations should be interpreted in light of the study’s post hoc design and further studies are needed to explore the causal link between the age at diagnosis of hyperlipidemia and metabolic disease progression.

This study has several notable strengths. Although exploratory in nature, it leverages data from one of the largest clinical trials of fenofibrate therapy, with an extended follow-up period that enables robust and granular assessment of long-term cardiovascular and mortality outcomes. Several factors also limit the interpretation of these data. First, age at hyperlipidemia diagnosis was retrospectively determined based on the self-reported year of diagnosis and should be interpreted as an approximate indicator. Given the often asymptomatic nature of hyperlipidemia and potential delays in medical evaluation, residual recall bias and misclassification cannot be excluded. Second, an inherent limitation is the missing data on hyperlipidemia duration in the ACCORD database, which amalgamates patients with no disease history and those with an unknown or unreliable diagnosis date, thereby restricting more nuanced subgroup analyses. Third, hyperlipidemia in ACCORD was captured as a physician-diagnosed clinical condition without phenotypic subclassification or standardized biochemical criteria. As a result, the present analyses reflect the clinical context of dyslipidemia commonly encountered in patients with type 2 diabetes and do not permit differentiation among specific lipid phenotypes. Fourth, this is a post hoc analysis of a randomized controlled trial, and these tests were not corrected for multiplicity; therefore, there is an increased risk of chance findings. Finally, the current study was conducted among North American (US and Canadian) patients with diabetes; therefore, the generalizability of our results might be limited.

Conclusion

In this exploratory post hoc analysis, fenofibrate use was associated with a reduced risk of cardiovascular events, cardiovascular death, and all-cause mortality in diabetes patients with early-diagnosed (≤ 60 years old) hyperlipidemia. Prospective studies and randomized controlled trials specifically designed to assess the modifying role of age at hyperlipidemia diagnosis on fenofibrate treatment effects are warranted to confirm and extend these findings.
